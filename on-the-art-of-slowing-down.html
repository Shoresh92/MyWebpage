On the Art of Slowing Down

January 2025  ·  5 min read

There is a particular kind of exhaustion that comes not from doing too much, but from never truly stopping. We have become so fluent in the language of productivity that stillness feels like a foreign dialect — one we once knew but have slowly, quietly forgotten.

I noticed it first in the mornings. I had taken to checking my phone before my feet touched the floor. Before coffee, before light, before even a full breath — the world was already pouring in. Notifications, headlines, messages. The day began not with me, but with everyone else.

The fastest way to lose yourself is to keep moving without ever asking where you're going.

The Permission We Never Give Ourselves

Slowing down, I've come to believe, is not a luxury. It is not something you earn after the inbox is cleared or the deadlines are met. Those conditions never fully arrive. Slowness has to be chosen — stubbornly, repeatedly, against the current.

It starts with small things. Leaving your phone in another room. Eating without a screen. Walking somewhere without headphones. These feel almost transgressive at first, as if you are breaking a social contract no one explicitly signed but everyone somehow agreed to.

But then something shifts. The world doesn't collapse. In fact, it sharpens. Colors seem more saturated. Conversations go deeper. Your own thoughts — the quiet ones that rarely get airtime — begin to surface.

What You Find in the Stillness

I won't pretend the stillness is always comfortable. Sometimes what rises is exactly what the busyness was designed to keep submerged: grief, longing, questions without easy answers. Slowing down can feel, initially, like opening a door you had carefully kept shut.

But on the other side of that discomfort is something worth having. Clarity. A sense of your own rhythm. The quiet knowledge of what actually matters to you, as distinct from what the world insists should.

We are, most of us, moving too fast to find out who we are when we stop. That might be worth changing.
